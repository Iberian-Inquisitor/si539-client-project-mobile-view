During Your Experience

Manage Your Engaged Learning Experience

During an engaged learning experience, students manage projects, communicate with partners, respond to challenges, and reflect on their learning. The work may change as your team learns more about the partner's needs and the people affected by the project. Continue checking your assumptions, documenting decisions, and connecting your daily tasks to the goals you established at the beginning.

Key Skills During the Experience

01

Communicate with Partners

Share updates clearly, ask questions, listen carefully, and confirm that everyone understands the project goals. Provide partners with timely information about progress, decisions, risks, and changes in scope. Use accessible language, avoid unnecessary technical terms, and make space for the partner to give feedback before your team moves too far in one direction.

02

Manage Projects and Teams

Track responsibilities, organize deadlines, and communicate with teammates when project needs change. Break large deliverables into smaller tasks, assign clear owners, and review progress regularly. Keep shared notes about decisions and next steps so that everyone can participate, understand the current status, and recognize when the original plan needs to be adjusted.

03

Respond to Challenges

Engaged learning projects may include unexpected problems. Use feedback, ethical decision-making, and collaborative problem-solving to move forward. Pause to identify what changed, who may be affected, and what information is still missing before choosing a response. Discuss options with your teammates and partner, consider possible risks or unintended consequences, and revise the plan when evidence shows that a different approach will create better value.

04

Reflect on Learning

Consider how the experience is changing your skills, perspective, understanding of the community, and approach to future projects. Reflect throughout the project instead of waiting until the final deliverable. Record moments of uncertainty, feedback that changed your thinking, and skills you are developing. Connect these observations to your learning goals and use them to make more thoughtful decisions during the remaining work.

Resources to Keep Your Project Moving

  • Project Management Worksheet: Revisit the scope, roles, and timeline with your team when priorities change. Record the updates and confirm them with your partner.
  • Project Scoping Handout & Terms: Return to this guidance when a new request changes the work your team agreed to do.
  • Email the Engaged Learning Office for guidance: Describe your project stage, the challenge, and the help you need. For course-specific questions, also contact your instructor.

Reflect and Hand Off Your Work

As your experience ends, use your project notes to review what you learned and prepare a useful handoff.

  1. Confirm the handoff: Agree with your partner on the final files, instructions, and any remaining limitations.
  2. Review your learning: Compare your original goals with the skills you practiced, feedback you received, and results you can describe.
  3. Share responsibly: Ask permission before using partner materials or project results in a portfolio or presentation.
